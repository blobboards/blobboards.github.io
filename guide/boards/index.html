Authoring a Board

This guide is about making a printable calibration board with blob_board: setting up the environment, worked examples, output types, printing and reproducing a board later. Every method form and every keyword (defaults, units, valid values) is listed once, in the blob_board reference. This guide shows them in use but does not repeat that list.

Setup

Board authoring is part of BlobBoards: using BlobBoards is all it needs. The package writes the PDF print master itself (no Cairo, no Ghostscript), and its TIFF and PNG conversions with Poppler and libtiff, which it brings along.

julia --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'           # once, in a checkout
julia --project=. examples/generate/board.jl                   # A4, for a 300 dpi printer, PDF

(Until 2026-10-14 authoring was an extension loaded by using CairoMakie.)

Your first board

using BlobBoards      # re-exports the units: mm, inch, px, dpi, …bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4"), seed = 0xBEEF, dir = "./output")

blob_sizes is required: the smallest and largest printed disc diameters. suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4") gives both for a 600 dpi printer and the default A4 board: 6 mm, the smallest disc that prints well, and 38 mm, 20% of the board's shorter side (see Blobs, below). This writes two files named after the board's uid:

  • blob_board_<uid>.pdf: the print master, PDF/X-4.
  • blob_board_<uid>.json: the sidecar. It records the configuration and every blob, which is enough to reproduce or re-render the board and to detect it.

The returned BoardBundle holds the same data, so with dir left out nothing is written and you get the bundle in memory. seed makes the placement reproducible; leave it out and a random seed is drawn and recorded.

Physical layout: a board has no density

A board is laid out in physical units, on a fixed grid of 1/7200 inch (0.01 pt, 3.5 µm): every blob centre, margin and frame edge, and the page itself, is a whole number of grid units. It has no pixel density. The print master is a vector PDF that prints on any printer, and nothing in the file or printed on the sheet mentions a density.

A density appears in two places only, and neither is part of the board:

  • A raster output has one. Tiff(600dpi) and Png(300dpi) are the sheet converted to pixels at that density (see Output, below). It is recorded in the JSON's render block, not in the board's configuration.
  • The printer enters through blob_sizes. The smallest disc that prints well depends on the printer's density, and suggest_blob_sizes(density, extent) gives it (see Blobs, below). Only the resulting length is stored.

The grid is the print master's. Every coordinate is written to the PDF as an exact two-decimal number of points, up to 9999.99 pt (3.53 m, the largest side a board may have; the grid was sized for a writer that kept 6 significant digits). Every grid value therefore reaches the PDF unchanged: the sidecar's blob centre 141.73 (pt) is the number the PDF holds. 7200 is also the least common multiple of 300, 600, 720, 1200, 1440 and 2400 dpi, so pixel edges at those densities fall on grid lines. The sidecar stores the pattern and its blobs in points ("unit": "pt") and declares the grid ("layout_grid": {"value": 0.01, "unit": "pt"}).

Boards stored in schema v3 are laid out on the printer's pixels at their own density. They load, re-render and rebuild exactly as printed, under their stored identity, and convert to a raster at that density only.

Page and board size

The page (canvas) can be a paper name, a width and height, or a Size2. The board can be smaller than the page; it is then centred, with white margins:

sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, (180mm, 180mm))    # 6 mm … 36 mm: suits every board belowbundle = blob_board("Letter"; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output")          # a paper name (BlobBoards.PRINT_SIZES)bundle = blob_board(200mm, 280mm; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output")      # width × heightbundle = blob_board(8inch, 10inch; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output")     # inches work toobundle = blob_board(Size2(width = 200mm, height = 280mm); blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output")# a 180 mm square board centred on an A4 pagebundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes,    board_size = Size2(width = 180mm, height = 180mm), dir = "./output")

The layout is two nested rectangles: the canvas (the page) and the board, centred on it — the canvas less twice the margin (a Size2: width left and right, height above and below; or give board_size instead, not both), which the blobs fill around the frame's marks (see Frame and ruler, below). By default the board leaves a 10 mm white margin on every side of the page: office printers cannot print the outer few millimetres of a sheet (3–6.35 mm; the campus Ricoh 4.23 mm), and anything there — the corner glyphs, the QR code, the ruler's ends — would be cut off. The margin also holds the id label under the board. The canvas is always the FINISHED sheet: for a print shop that trims, keep the canvas and the margin and add a trim allowance to the PDF with Pdf(bleed = 2mm) (see Page boxes and bleed). A zero margin makes the board the whole sheet, with nothing left for the id label. The board records its margin, and its QR code carries it.

Blobs

blob_sizes = (min, max) are the smallest and largest printed disc diameters, as physical lengths. It is required and both ends are stated: leaving it out, or giving nothing for either end, is an ArgumentError. suggest_blob_sizes(density, extent) gives both ends, the smallest from the printer and the largest from the board:

# a 600 dpi printer and the default A4 board (190 × 277 mm inside its 10 mm margins): 6 mm … 38 mmbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4"), seed = 0x1234, dir = "./output")# the board's extent as a Size2 or a (width, height) tuplesizes = suggest_blob_sizes(1200dpi, (300mm, 200mm))      # (3.0 mm, 40.0 mm)# printed discs from 2 mm to 12 mm acrossbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (2mm, 12mm), dir = "./output")# the max as a fraction of the pattern's short sidebundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (4.0mm, 0.2short_side), dir = "./output")# light blobs on a black backgroundbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (4.0mm, 0.2short_side), polarity = :light, dir = "./output")

The smallest disc is a choice about the printer, which is why it has no default. suggest_blob_sizes(density, extent)[1] is the smallest diameter that prints its profile well at a printer's density: 3 mm at 1200 dpi, inversely proportional to the density.

Printersmallest disc
300 dpi12 mm
600 dpi6 mm
1200 dpi3 mm

A printer makes grey from halftone cells, small blocks of printer dots, so the cells grow as the density drops, and a disc must span enough of them for its profile to print smoothly. At 600 dpi this matches a measured office laser printer: discs from about 5–6 mm up reproduce the profile within ~10 grey levels of 255, while smaller ones print their mid-tones too light (examples/print_quality). A smaller min is accepted; the suggestion is not a limit. The rule's two numbers are min_blob_diameter_mm and min_blob_reference_dpi in configs/authoring.toml.

The largest disc is a length or a fraction of the pattern's short side. suggest_blob_sizes(density, extent)[2] is 20% of the board's shorter side, but never more than 64 mm: on a large sheet a fixed fraction is a very big disc (20% of an A0 board is 164 mm). A paper name as the extent stands for the board blob_board makes on it by default, the paper less 10 mm on every side.

Board's shorter sidelargest disc
128 mm (A5 board)25.6 mm
190 mm (A4 board)38 mm
320 mm and up64 mm

0.2short_side is instead exactly that fraction of the pattern's short side (the board's: a board's pattern is the whole board), with no cap, and any length (12mm) is taken as given; the largest disc must be smaller than the pattern's short side. The cap exists only inside suggest_blob_sizes. Its two numbers are max_blob_fraction and max_blob_diameter_mm in configs/authoring.toml.

The placement keywords (scales_per_octave, α, cutoff, max_trials) are described in the reference and the Patterns guide.

Frame and ruler

┌──────┬───────────────────┬─────────┐
│  QR  │    North ruler    │  uid 1  │
├──────┘                   └─────────┤
│ West                          East │
│ruler    blobs fill the     ruler   │
│         whole board                │
├──────┐                   ┌─────────┤
│uid 2 │    South ruler    │  uid 3  │
└──────┴───────────────────┴─────────┘

The blobs are placed on the whole board. Everything else printed on it — the frame's marks — is a reserved rectangle that placement keeps clear of. There are two, and each is switched by its size, a zero length (in any unit) turning it off:

  • corner_size (10 mm by default): the four corner cells — the QR code top-left, a uid character in each other corner. Blobs keep 1 mm clear of them (more than the QR code's own quiet zone, which is inside its cell);
  • ruler_width (10 mm by default): the four ruler bands along the sides, between the cells. Blobs may touch a band.

So ruler_width = 0mm keeps only the corner cells, corner_size = 0mm only the ruler, and both at 0mm give a board of blobs alone. The two are independent — a narrow ruler with large corner cells is corner_size = 14mm, ruler_width = 6mm — and the blob counts are taken from the board's area less the reserved one. The tick keywords belong to the ruler: with it off they are refused. On a :light board the marks are painted white over the black background.

The QR code carries the board's definition as a short text, the board code (BlobBoards.board_code):

BB4/210MM/297MM/10MM/10MM/10MM/10MM/0MM/10MM/5MM/10MM/5MM/A4/4.25196850394/26.929/3/5000/1.2/2/D/0D435510

— the format, page size and margin, ruler width, corner size, padding, ruler ticks, seed (hex), the smallest and largest σ (pt), the ladder and placement settings, polarity, and the first 8 hex of the identity as a check. A length is a number and its unit, the unit symbol of VisualGeometryCore's FlexUnits registry upper-cased so the code stays in QR's alphanumeric set: 210MM, or 8.5INCH on Letter, whichever of mm and inch is shorter and reads back to the identical value. The reader lower-cases the symbol and resolves it through the registry, so any registered length unit reads (21CM); a number without a unit is refused, and so is a code in the older mm/IN spelling. Any phone reads it (a 41 × 41 code, its modules about 0.24 mm in the 10 mm corner); BlobBoards.parse_board_code turns it back into the board's configuration, refusing a code whose recomputed identity disagrees with the check, so a print alone rebuilds its board. A board's identity covers its schema, its configuration and the blobs that configuration places, so the check also proves the rebuild placed the same blobs: a code decoded by software that places differently is refused, never rebuilt into another board. BB4 is the board schema's version; a change to placement or the frame's marks is a new schema. If the QR code does not fit its corner, it is omitted with a warning. The ruler's ticks sit at exact multiples of their spacing (to the 0.01 pt grid; within 3 µm in the print master) and are drawn 0.1 mm wide.

# both marks (the default), a wider ruler, ticks every 10 mm / 5 mmbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (4.0mm, 0.2short_side), ruler_width = 15mm,    major_tick_spacing = 10mm, minor_tick_spacing = 5mm, dir = "./output")# a narrow ruler and large corner cellsbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (4.0mm, 0.2short_side), corner_size = 14mm, ruler_width = 6mm, dir = "./output")# no ruler: blobs fill the board around the four corner cells (QR, uid)bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4"), ruler_width = 0mm, dir = "./output")# no marks at all: blobs fill the whole boardbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4"), corner_size = 0mm, ruler_width = 0mm, dir = "./output")

frame_padding adds white space between a mark's content (ticks, QR code, uid glyphs) and its edges. A stored v3 board keeps the layout it was printed with: a band around an inset pattern, the corner cells where the bands cross (its :none is a blank band).

Ruler widths

major_tick_width and minor_tick_width are each tick's full drawn length across the band, centred in it. By default the major ticks fill the padded band (major_tick_width = ruler_width - 2frame_padding) and the minor ticks are half as long. Widths must satisfy 0 < minor_tick_width ≤ major_tick_width ≤ ruler_width - 2frame_padding; anything else is an ArgumentError.

# 15 mm band; major ticks 8 mm long, minor ticks 4 mm, centred in the bandbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (4.0mm, 0.2short_side), ruler_width = 15mm,    major_tick_width = 8mm, minor_tick_width = 4mm, dir = "./output")

Output

The output keyword says what the board is written as. It is a value that carries exactly the options its format has:

outputFileOptionsDensity
Pdf() (default)the print master, PDF/X-4shading_function = :calculator, icc_profile = :linear, bleed = 0mmnone
Tiff(density)the sheet converted to an 8-bit grey TIFFicc_profile = :linearthe image's, e.g. Tiff(600dpi)
Png(density)the sheet converted to a grey PNG, for digital use—the image's, e.g. Png(300dpi)
sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4")bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output")                             # Pdf()bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output", output = Pdf(shading_function = :sampled))bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output", output = Pdf(bleed = 2mm))      # print-shop bleedbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output", output = Tiff(600dpi))bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes, dir = "./output", output = Png(300dpi))

The output is checked before placement, and output without a dir is an ArgumentError. A raster's density is pixels per inch of the image, a whole number. Three rules apply to a raster and none to a PDF:

  • the board's smallest blob must be at least 2 pixels per σ at the density (the error names the density that would do);
  • a stored v3 board converts at its own density only;
  • a sheet over 32745 px a side cannot be converted.

PDF (default)

bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4"), dir = "./output")   # PDF/X-4
  • always PDF/X-4 (what most print shops ask for). A PDF/X-4 master is also PDF/A-2b, so veraPDF (which has no PDF/X profile) can validate it: verapdf --flavour 2b board.pdf. For the PDF/X side itself, ask the print shop to preflight the file (most do, with callas pdfToolbox or Enfocus PitStop).
  • an OutputIntent declaring the print condition (linear-reflectance grey by default; see ICC profiles below)
  • page boxes: the TrimBox is the finished sheet, and an optional bleed around it (see Page boxes and bleed)
  • the blobs as vector shadings (below)
  • vector rulers, and the text (the uid characters in the corners, the id label) set in one embedded font, so the page is searchable and the label's hash can be copied; the file holds no image

BlobBoards writes the file directly: one page with a Flate-compressed content stream, and one shared radial shading painted once per blob.

Page boxes and bleed

A PDF page carries nested boxes. Viewers show the MediaBox (the page); a print shop cuts to the TrimBox (the finished sheet); the BleedBox says how far printing may run past the cut. BlobBoards sets them from the canvas and Pdf(; bleed):

│ │   ┌────────────── board (190 × 277 mm) ───────────┐   │ │
│ │   │ [QR]                                    [uid] │   │ │
│ │   │                     blobs                     │   │ │
│ │   │ [uid]                                   [uid] │   │ │

│ └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ │
│                  bleed (2 mm, white)                      │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  • What you pass is the finished size. "A4" or blob_board(1000mm, 1000mm; …) is the sheet after trimming; margin is measured from the trim. The bleed adds a white strip outside it, so a cut that is slightly off never shows.
  • What tools report. A viewer's page size is the MediaBox: the 1 m board with 2 mm bleed reads 1004 × 1004 mm. Preflight tools and RIPs show the 1000 × 1000 mm TrimBox and cut there. Tell the shop "finished size 1000 × 1000 mm, 2 mm bleed included".
  • Bleed does not change the board. It is a rendering option, like id_label: the blobs, uid, QR code and file name are those of the same board without bleed. The JSON's render block records it ("bleed": {"value": 2, "unit": "mm"}), so rebuild_board prints the same page. It is rounded to the layout grid (0.01 pt).
  • PDF only. A Tiff or Png is the sheet without bleed: a raster has no page boxes.
  • Size limit. A page side (the canvas plus twice the bleed) may not exceed 9999.99 pt (about 3.53 m); a larger one is refused before placement.
  • Do not fake it. Enlarging the canvas and the margin yourself (a 214 × 301 mm canvas with a 12 mm margin) puts the TrimBox on the larger sheet, and the board gets a different uid.

Until 2026-10-16 the TrimBox marked the board inside the margins, so a shop trimming to it cut away the margin and the id label. Boards written before then still have that TrimBox; rebuild_board writes the corrected one.

Blob encoding

A board's blobs are always vector graphics — its one source of truth: one radial shading per blob, whose colour function is the blob profile (the Gaussian truncated at the cutoff disc and normalised to 0 at the centre and 1 at the rim). Pdf(; shading_function) picks how that function is written:

shading_functionThe profile isUse
:calculator (default)a PostScript calculator function (the exact formula)everything; the printer evaluates the Gaussian at its own resolution
:sampleda 1024-entry sampled tablea RIP that mishandles calculator functions

A vector board is small at any size — the file grows with the number of blobs, not the area (a 1 m² board: ~0.25 MB, against ~83 MB as a 1200 dpi raster) — and has no size limit. Every raster of a board (TIFF, PNG, the detection gallery's images) is a conversion of this PDF; blob_pattern, which makes pattern images directly, samples the same profile at pixel centres, and the two agree to within one 8-bit level.

How it looks on screen depends on the viewer. A renderer that evaluates the shading per pixel (Cairo — pdftocairo, which rasterize_pdf uses) reproduces the profile within one grey level at every size; Ghostscript, MuPDF and poppler's own renderer draw shadings in ~1-device-pixel bands, which steps blobs only a few pixels wide (σ = 2 px: tens of levels) and is invisible for large ones (σ ≥ 47 px: ≤ 5 levels). What the printer does is the RIP's: check a test print.

TIFF and PNG are the PDF converted at their own density, so Tiff and Png have no shading_function. The function written is recorded in the JSON's render block, and a reconstruction reproduces it; a JSON recording none rebuilds as :calculator.

TIFF

For print shops and photo labs that take raster files (a print output only: the loader never reads a TIFF, and renders a board kept as one from its JSON):

bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (3mm, 0.2short_side), output = Tiff(1200dpi), dir = "./output")
  • the whole sheet converted from the vector PDF at the TIFF's density, 8-bit grey: the page rounded up to whole pixels, so a pixel coordinate is the page's points × dpi/72 (blob centres are not pixel centres)
  • the density in the resolution tags, so the print software sizes it exactly
  • the ICC profile embedded (Tiff(density; icc_profile), linear reflectance by default)
  • LZW compression with the horizontal predictor

PNG

For digital use (rendering, training):

bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = (12mm, 0.2short_side), output = Png(300dpi), dir = "./output")

The PNG is single-channel grey with linear gamma (file_gamma = 1.0) and carries no ICC profile.

ICC profiles

A PDF/X file's OutputIntent says how its grey values are to be printed. The default, :linear, declares them linear reflectance: a value of 0.5 reflects half the light of bare paper, so a Gaussian in the file is a Gaussian in the light the camera sees. A colour-managed RIP (print shops) honours it; an office printer ignores it and applies its own tone curve.

sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(600dpi, "A4")bundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes, output = Pdf(icc_profile = :linear), dir = "./output")   # the defaultbundle = blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = sizes, output = Pdf(icc_profile = "/path/to/press_gray.icc"), dir = "./output")

Tiff(density; icc_profile) embeds the same profile; a PNG has none.

Identity Label

Every new board prints its identity in one 6 pt line under the board, in the sheet margin (inside the trimmed sheet, clear of the printer's unprintable border):

blob_board_0d4_35510 · BB4/210MM/297MM/10MM/10MM/10MM/10MM/0MM/10MM/5MM/10MM/5MM/A4/4.25196850394/26.929/3/5000/1.2/2/D/0D435510 · BlobBoards 1.1.2

The middle part is the board code, the same string the QR code holds: parse_board_code rebuilds the board from it. It is text in the PDF, so it can be searched for and copied. The label mentions no density. (A stored v3 board's label keeps its hash, size and density.)

Every printed sheet — frameless boards included — can so be traced to its JSON. The label is a rendering option, not part of the board's identity: id_label = false turns it off without changing the UID. A board that fills its sheet has no room for it, and the label is skipped (with a warning for a frameless board, which would otherwise carry no printed identity). The JSON's render block records whether it was drawn: BlobBoards.rebuild_board and the gallery's regeneration rebuild the sheet as recorded, and a board with no render block (written before 0.36.2) rebuilds without one. render_board defaults to false.

Printing

A board is a measuring instrument, so it must be printed at exactly 100%.

  • In the print dialog choose "Actual size" / 100%. Turn off "Fit", "Shrink oversized pages" and "Scale to printable area". An A4 page is larger than any printer's printable area, so a dialog left on "Fit" shrinks the whole board by about 3%. PDF/X-4 output asks Acrobat/Reader for actual size (/PrintScaling /None); other viewers may ignore it.
  • If your printer cannot print to the page edge and keeps scaling, make the board smaller than the page: a wider margin leaves more white, so the page prints at 100% without anything being clipped.
  • Print grey / black ink only, at the highest quality, with image enhancement off.
  • Check every print. Measure the rulers: the major ticks are major_tick_spacing apart, so at the default spacing ten intervals must span exactly 100.0 mm. On a frameless board, measure the pattern edge to edge. A "fit" print comes out about 3% short.
  • Print shops print PDF/X at 100% by default and cut to the TrimBox, the finished sheet.

Print-shop bleed

A print shop that trims its sheets asks for a trim allowance ("2 mm bleed on every side"). Give it to the PDF, not to the board:

blob_board("A4"; blob_sizes = suggest_blob_sizes(1200dpi, "A4"), output = Pdf(bleed = 2mm), dir = "./out")

See Page boxes and bleed for what the boxes hold and what the shop sees.

Reproducing a board

The JSON sidecar is the board's definition. There are three ways to use it.

# 1. Load the bundle (and its image): the input to detection and coordinatesbundle, image = BlobBoards.load("./output/blob_board_<uid>.json")# 2. Re-render the stored board (its recorded blobs, frame, QR and uid);#    no placement is re-run, so it matches the printed board exactly.#    The output is the first argument; `dir` is required.BlobBoards.render_board(bundle; dir = "./rerender", output = Png(300dpi))BlobBoards.render_board(bundle; dir = "./rerender")                        # Pdf() by default# 3. Re-generate from the configuration (placement is re-run from the seed)bundle = BlobBoards.rebuild_board("./output/blob_board_<uid>.json"; dir = "./output2")

Form 3 writes the board as its JSON's render block records (output and id_label); a keyword overrides it, e.g. output = Tiff(600dpi).

Reading a board back

bundle, image = BlobBoards.load("./output/blob_board_<uid>.json")blobs(bundle, :pattern; units = mm)                  # mm, pattern frameblobs(bundle, :board; units = mm)                    # mm, board frame (includes the frame band)blobs(bundle, :canvas; density = 300dpi)       # px of a 300 dpi conversion (COLMAP convention)blobs(bundle, :world)                                # centred, y-up, unitless mm: the z = 0 calibration planeboard_config(bundle).ruler_width     # the BoardConfigpattern_config(bundle).seed          # the PatternConfig

A board has no density, so units = px (the default) needs the density of the pixels you ask for, density; without it blobs is an ArgumentError. See Coordinate Systems for the reference frames and transforms.

Render a STORED board as output — Pdf() (the default), Tiff(density) or Png(density) — into dir: draw the bundle's recorded blobs (vector shadings) and its frame, QR and uid glyphs, without re-running

short_side

Marker for a size relative to the pattern's short side: 0.2short_side is 20% of it.

suggest_blob_sizes(density, extent) -> (smallest, largest)

The blob_sizes of a board printed on a printer of density (e.g. 600dpi): the smallest and largest printed disc DIAMETERS, as a tuple of lengths in mm.
